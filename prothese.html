🦴 Shoulder Prosthesis – Glenoid Component & Finite Element Modelling

Objective

The objective of this project is to study the mechanical behaviour of a cemented glenoid component used in shoulder prostheses. The goal is to better understand stress distribution, micromotions at the bone–implant interface, and potential failure mechanisms under physiological loading, using both numerical simulations and experimental testing.

Tools & Languages
  • Python
  • Abaqus
  • Catia

State of the Art – Glenoid Components

Before designing and modelling a new glenoid implant, we conducted an in-depth literature review on:

  • Biomechanical testing of shoulder prostheses and glenoid components
  • Finite element models of implanted scapulae and glenoid fixation systems
  • Failure mechanisms, micromotion, and cement fatigue in glenoid implants

The report below summarizes key experimental setups, modelling strategies, and design guidelines that inform our own conception and finite element study.

Finite Element Modelling

Based on the state-of-the-art, we are developing a finite element model of a shoulder glenoid component to analyse stress distributions, micromotions at the bone–implant interface, and the influence of design parameters.

At this stage, we are focusing on:

  • Reconstructing the scapula and implant geometry
  • Defining realistic boundary conditions and load cases
  • Comparing different fixation concepts using numerical simulations

The animation shows one of our current finite element configurations under physiological loading.

Experimental Work – Mechanical Testing

To validate the mechanical stability of the cemented glenoid component, we carried out a complete experimental campaign:

  • Preparation of the polyurethane foam block
  • Cementing and alignment of the implant
  • Calibration of the optical displacement system
  • Static and cyclic mechanical loading using an Instron machine
